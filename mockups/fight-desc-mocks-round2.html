MOCK DThe Wire — story first

Inverts today's design. The timestamped play-by-play — currently hidden behind a click — becomes the card itself, running down a live-ticker time rail like an ESPN Gamecast. Ordinary exchanges are small and muted; significant moments get a colored node and brighter text; knockdowns and the finish break out into full-width impact cards. The stats you lead with today collapse into one tiny agate line per round.

Trade-off: tallest of all the options and it lives or dies on event-text quality — but nothing else makes the fight feel like it *happened*. Pairs naturally with collapsing R1/R2 by default on long fights.

MOCK EThe Gazette Fight Report

Leaves the stat-panel genre entirely: the fight renders as a press clipping from the game's own Gazette — a headline the engine composes from the result, an italic deck, a two-column report with a drop cap, a pull quote, and the round stats where a newspaper would put them: an agate box score at the bottom. Ties the fight into the media/notoriety side of the game's world; a red corner stamp marks the verdict.

Trade-off: needs headline/lede template writing (same template system as the commentary), and the light "paper" panel is a deliberate contrast inside the dark app — a statement piece, not a subtle one. Instantly screenshot-worthy.

MARINO STOPS BRYANT IN THREE

A second-round knockdown flips the fight; the referee waves it off at 2:41 of the third.

CAGESIDE · FIGHT No. 6 · MARINO 4–1 DEF. BRYANT 6–2

For five minutes it was the Deshawn Bryant fight everyone expected — forward pressure, a takedown, a control clinic. Momo Marino ended the opening round down on every card and looking short of answers.

The answer arrived early in the second: a short left hook that put Bryant on the canvas and the fight on new terms. From there Marino dictated — a takedown of his own, heavy top pressure, a 10–8 round by any honest reckoning.

"Bryant beat the count — but he never truly recovered from the hook."

In the third, Bryant swung wide and desperate. Marino countered clean, trapped him against the fence, and rallied with unanswered hooks until the referee had seen enough at 2:41.
